# Radio Night — Season 1, Episode 1: "The Sitting"

> **Spoiler warning.** This document contains the complete solution of the mystery, including who did it. If you have not played Radio Night yet, play it first at https://radionight.app.

## 1. The World

**Town:** Millhaven, New Hampshire. Population roughly 4,000. A tired mill town that never quite recovered from the 1970s. One main street. One diner. One decent inn. A place where people know each other's business and mostly pretend they don't.

**The Bridgewater Inn:** A two-storey clapboard inn on Main Street. Ground floor: a public bar (The Tap Room) and a small dining room. Upstairs: six guest rooms. Out back: a kitchen. The innkeeper lives in a converted storeroom off the kitchen. The detective uses a private snug off the bar for interviews — known locally as "the back room."

**The time:** Saturday night, October 17, 1981. The painter is found dead in his room (Room 5, upstairs) at approximately 10:45 PM. The detective arrives by 11:15 PM. The game takes place between 11:15 PM and the small hours of Sunday morning.

## 2. Characters

### The Victim
**Name:** Gerald Fenn  
**Age:** 58  
**Role:** Portrait painter. Respected locally, difficult personally. Staying at the inn for three weeks while painting a commissioned portrait.  
**Personality:** Perceptive, sardonic, self-serving. Uses observation as leverage. Not violent but uses information like a blade.  
**How he dies:** Struck from behind with a heavy glass ashtray (found in the room, wiped). Died quickly. Death occurred between 9:45 PM and 10:15 PM.  
**He is not the subject of sympathy, but he did not deserve to die.**

---

### Suspect 1 — The Killer
**Name:** Ray Kowalski  
**Age:** 44  
**Role:** Bartender at the Bridgewater Inn. Has worked there eleven years. Grew up in Millhaven.  
**Personality:** Warm, unhurried, competent. The kind of person everyone likes without thinking about why. Protective instinct runs deep — almost to a fault.  
**Motive:** Ray was quietly helping Carol Wentworth plan her departure from her husband, Dwight. Gerald Fenn discovered this (he overheard a conversation in the bar) and confronted Ray privately on Saturday afternoon — telling Ray he intended to tell Dwight unless Ray paid him to stay quiet. Ray met with Fenn in Room 5 to "settle it," the confrontation escalated, and Ray struck him.  
**The complication:** Ray genuinely believed Dwight was dangerous enough that Fenn telling him would put Carol in physical danger. This doesn't excuse the killing — but it explains why a decent man made a terrible choice.  
**Alibi he gives:** Claims he was behind the bar all evening. Two witnesses can partially support this — but there's a gap.  
**The truth:** He slipped upstairs at approximately 9:50 PM. Was back behind the bar by 10:20 PM. The gap is 25–30 minutes.  
**Voice direction:** Warm, steady, slightly too composed. A small pause before answering questions about the upstairs. Does not volunteer anything.

---

### Suspect 2 — Red Herring #1
**Name:** Dwight Wentworth  
**Age:** 52  
**Role:** Local businessman. Owns the Millhaven Lumber Supply. Commissioned Fenn's portrait of his wife.  
**Personality:** Controlling, status-conscious, prone to sudden cold anger. Doesn't shout — gets quiet instead.  
**Motive (apparent):** The portrait sittings have been going on for three weeks. Dwight suspects Fenn of inappropriate interest in Carol. He confronted Fenn on Friday night (heard by the innkeeper). He is also the kind of man who, once he learns his wife has been planning to leave him, would be capable of violence.  
**Alibi he gives:** Was at the bar from 9:00 PM until after 10:30. Several witnesses. But he was seen going upstairs briefly around 9:30 PM.  
**The truth:** He went upstairs to slip a threatening note under Fenn's door — not to confront him in person. He's frightened of Fenn's potential testimony about the marriage. The note is still in Room 5 (under the bed, not immediately visible). It is incriminating but not proof of murder.  
**Voice direction:** Clipped, formal, suspicious of the detective's motives. Uses "I" constantly. Mentions his standing in Millhaven.

---

### Suspect 3 — Red Herring #2
**Name:** Carol Wentworth  
**Age:** 38  
**Role:** Dwight's wife. Has been sitting for her portrait at their house. Came to the inn Saturday evening to speak privately with Ray.  
**Personality:** Quiet, watchful, precise with words. Has learned to be careful. Not a victim in the passive sense — she has a plan and she's working it.  
**Motive (apparent):** Fenn overheard her planning to leave Dwight and threatened to expose her. She had the most to lose from his interference.  
**Alibi she gives:** Was in the bar from 8:30 until approximately 9:15, then left. Went home. Didn't come back.  
**The truth:** She did leave at 9:15. She did not kill Fenn. She doesn't know Ray did.  
**Voice direction:** Measured, cooperative but careful. Answers exactly what's asked, no more. Will acknowledge her plans to leave if directly confronted — she has nothing to hide from the detective.  
**Agency note:** Carol is not a passive plot device. She made her own plan, executed it, and neither invited nor needed the killing. Her arc should feel complete independent of the crime.

---

### Witness 1
**Name:** Edna Marsh  
**Age:** 63  
**Role:** Innkeeper. Has owned the Bridgewater Inn for twenty years.  
**Personality:** Pragmatic, dry, deeply loyal to the inn's reputation. Observes everything from habit. Not unkind.  
**What she knows:** She saw Dwight go upstairs at 9:30 and come back down about five minutes later (the threatening note visit). She heard Fenn come down to the bar at around 8:45 to get a drink and go back up. She did NOT see Ray go upstairs — she was in the kitchen from approximately 9:40 onward. She found the body when she went up to check why a light was on under the door.  
**Voice direction:** Matter-of-fact, slightly impatient with imprecise questions. Answers precisely but doesn't editorialize.

---

### Witness 2
**Name:** Dennis Pruett  
**Age:** 55  
**Role:** Local regular. Retired mill foreman. Drinks at the Tap Room most Saturday evenings.  
**Personality:** Talky, well-meaning, unreliable about time. Genuinely wants to help. Gets details wrong in good faith.  
**What he knows:** He was at the bar all evening. He remembers Ray was there "most of the time." He thinks Ray "stepped away for a bit" around 10 but isn't sure when exactly. He saw Carol leave. He heard Dwight and Fenn exchange words on Friday — not Saturday.  
**Key unreliability:** He initially says Ray was there "the whole night." Under gentle follow-up he admits Ray was away "ten, maybe fifteen minutes." The real gap was closer to thirty.  
**Voice direction:** Friendly, enthusiastic, frequently self-correcting.

---

### The Narrator / Detective Voice
**Name:** The player is the detective. The narrator frames scenes and transitions.  
**Voice:** Calm, evocative, radio-noir register. Think late-night public radio. Not arch or campy.  
**Function:** Opens scenes, describes physical spaces, signals time passage, delivers the finale assessment.

## 4. The Clue Chain (Fair-Play Design)

The clues are tiered: early clues point to multiple suspects, later clues converge on Ray.

### Tier 1 — Available from scene entry
These are discoverable without conditions; they establish the situation and open multiple lines of inquiry.

| Clue ID | Where | What the detective finds | What it means |
|---|---|---|---|
| `clue-ashtray` | Room 5 | Heavy glass ashtray on the side table, wiped but slightly tacky | Murder weapon. Wiped suggests presence of mind, not panic. |
| `clue-whiskey-glass` | Room 5 | One empty glass. No second glass. | Fenn was alone when he had his last drink. No meeting yet, or meeting hadn't started. |
| `clue-note-under-bed` | Room 5 | Folded note under the bed. Reads: *"Stay away from my wife. This is your last warning. — D.W."* | Dwight threatened Fenn in writing. Incriminating. Not a murder clue. |
| `clue-back-stairs` | Hallway | A second staircase at the end of the hall leads to the kitchen | Two ways up. The killer may not have used the main stairs. |
| `clue-friday-argument` | Tap Room / any witness | Dwight and Fenn argued publicly the night before | Establishes Dwight's hostility. Tier 1 red herring. |

### Tier 2 — Unlocked after interviewing specific witnesses
Require at least one substantive conversation.

| Clue ID | Source | Unlock condition | Discovery |
|---|---|---|---|
| `clue-dwight-went-upstairs` | Edna | Interview Edna + ask about Saturday night movements | "Mr Wentworth went upstairs around half-nine. Back down inside five minutes." |
| `clue-carol-left-early` | Dennis or Edna | Interview either + ask about Carol | Carol left at 9:15. She was gone well before the death window. |
| `clue-ray-was-away` | Dennis | Interview Dennis + ask if Ray was behind the bar all night | "He stepped away for a bit. Ten, fifteen minutes, maybe. I wasn't watching the clock." (underestimate) |
| `clue-kitchen-sink` | Edna | Interview Edna + ask about after 9:30 | "I saw Ray in the kitchen around ten or a little after. I thought he'd come down for a glass. He washed his hands." |
| `clue-back-stairs-access` | Edna | Ask Edna who uses the back stairs | "Staff, mostly. Guests don't really know about them. Ray uses them to bring up ice." |

### Tier 3 — Unlocked after combining Tier 2 clues
These require the detective to have gathered earlier evidence and pursue the contradiction.

| Clue ID | Source | Unlock condition | Discovery |
|---|---|---|---|
| `clue-gap-contradiction` | Dennis | Has `clue-ray-was-away` AND confronts Dennis with the death window timing | Dennis reconsiders. "I suppose it could have been closer to half an hour. Maybe more. I just assumed he'd be back." |
| `clue-fenn-heard-carol` | Carol | Interview Carol + ask if Fenn knew about her plans | Carol: "He must have heard something. Ray told me Fenn had approached him. I didn't know about the money." |
| `clue-fenn-approach` | Ray | Interview Ray + ask if Fenn spoke to him privately | (First time): Ray denies any private conversation. (With `clue-fenn-heard-carol`): Ray goes quiet, then: "He asked me for money. I told him I'd think about it." |
| `clue-ray-admission-partial` | Ray | Has `clue-fenn-approach` AND `clue-gap-contradiction` AND confront Ray about the kitchen | Ray: "I went upstairs. To talk to him. That's all I'm going to say right now." |

### The Deduction
A player who has discovered:
- `clue-ray-was-away` + `clue-gap-contradiction` (Ray had the time)
- `clue-back-stairs-access` + `clue-kitchen-sink` (Ray had the route and was in the kitchen after)
- `clue-fenn-approach` or `clue-fenn-heard-carol` (Ray had the motive)
- `clue-ashtray` (murder weapon implies premeditated enough to wipe)

...can correctly accuse Ray Kowalski. The note from Dwight is a deliberate red herring — threatening but not murderous, and Edna's sighting of Dwight upstairs for only five minutes doesn't fit the longer death window.

## 7. Accusation Rubric

**Correct accusation (Ray Kowalski):**
Accepted if the player has discovered at least FOUR of these six clues:
- `clue-ray-was-away` (confirmed by Dennis)
- `clue-gap-contradiction` (Dennis revises the gap)
- `clue-kitchen-sink` (Edna's kitchen sighting)
- `clue-back-stairs-access` (route established)
- `clue-fenn-approach` OR `clue-fenn-heard-carol` (motive established)
- `clue-ray-admission-partial` (Ray admits going upstairs)

**Accepted endings:**
- `ending-correct-full` — player has all 6 clues. Ray breaks down. Full confession and emotional resolution.
- `ending-correct-partial` — player has 4–5 clues. Ray admits going upstairs but doesn't fully confess. Detective's assessment is confident; Ray is taken in.
- `ending-wrong-dwight` — player accuses Dwight. Played straight: Dwight is arrested, protests, the note makes him look guilty — but the finale reveals the gap in the timeline that exonerates him, and Ray is picked up later. Bittersweet — Carol's situation doesn't improve immediately.
- `ending-wrong-carol` — player accuses Carol. Carol produces an alibi corroborated by a neighbor. Wrong accusation acknowledged; the real killer goes free (for now).

**Minimum condition to accuse:**
Player must have spoken to at least 3 of the 4 living witnesses/suspects AND discovered at least 2 named clues. Cannot accuse on a hunch with no evidence.

---

### 7a. Accusation as implemented

- **Who can be accused:** the three primary suspects (Ray, Dwight, Carol). Edna and Dennis are witnesses.
- **When:** after speaking with at least **three** people (the first draft said "3 of the 4 living witnesses/suspects", but there are five) and finding at least two clues.
- **What the player puts forward:** the player chooses at least two discovered clues as their evidence. The ending is graded on the evidence **presented**, not on everything the detective holds. Irrelevant extra evidence is not penalised.
- **The six steps of the case against Ray** (the debrief shows these after a correct accusation):
  1. Ray left the bar around ten
  2. He was gone far longer than he said
  3. He washed his hands in the kitchen afterward
  4. He knew the back stairs
  5. Fenn had a hold over him (either `clue-fenn-approach` or `clue-fenn-heard-carol`)
  6. He admitted going up to Fenn's room
- **Right suspect:** all six steps established gives the full ending; anything less gives the partial ending. *Departure from the first draft:* the draft implied an accusation with fewer than four clues would not be "accepted". With no ending written for that case, a thin but correct accusation now gets the partial ending, and the assessment says the case was thin.
- **Wrong suspect:** the case closes with the matching wrong ending. There is no retry within a run; "Investigate again" starts a fresh one.
- **Refusals reveal nothing:** an accusation made too early is refused with the same message for every suspect.
- **Endings written for this build:** `ending-correct-partial` and `ending-wrong-carol` did not exist in the first draft. They are in `beats.ts` and are open to revision.

## 8. Scripted Beats (Must Be Prewritten and Prerecorded)

These are the five moments that define the game's emotional shape. They are authored dialogue — the LLM does not improvise them.

### Beat 1: Opening narration
*Narrator, over the sound of rain and a distant phone ringing:*
> "Millhaven, New Hampshire. October 17th, 1981. Just past eleven at night, and the rain isn't letting up. Someone called the station from a payphone on Main Street — a woman's voice, steady but not calm. Gerald Fenn, portrait painter, found dead in Room 5 of the Bridgewater Inn. Single blow to the head. The bar was still open when it happened. That means people were downstairs. That means someone knows something."

### Beat 2: First entry to Room 5
*Narrator:*
> "Room 5. The bed's been slept in but not for tonight. A suitcase sits open on the chair — half packed, or half unpacked; hard to say. On the side table: a glass, a lamp still burning, and a glass ashtray that's been moved recently. You can tell because of the ring it left on the wood. Gerald Fenn is on the floor near the window. He didn't fall there. Someone put him there, or he tried to crawl."

### Beat 3: Carol's disclosure (Tier 3 trigger)
*Carol, when asked directly about her plans and Fenn's knowledge:*
> "Yes. I was leaving. I had a date, I had a plan, I had a bag in Ray's storeroom. Dwight doesn't know — didn't know. Gerald Fenn apparently did. Ray told me on Saturday afternoon that Fenn had approached him. Said he'd keep quiet if Ray paid him. I told Ray not to. I said I wasn't afraid of Dwight finding out anymore. I meant it." *[Pause.]* "I didn't know about tonight. I want you to understand that. Whatever happened, I didn't ask for it."

### Beat 4: Ray's partial admission (Tier 3 trigger, final confrontation)
*Ray, when cornered with the full evidence chain:*
> "I went upstairs. I'm not going to pretend I didn't. I went up to tell him the deal was off — that there was no money, that he could do whatever he wanted. And then —" *[Long pause.]* "He laughed. He said Carol would never leave, that women like her never do. And I thought about what Dwight would do when he found out. What he's done before." *[Steadier now.]* "I'm not asking you to understand it. I'm asking you to believe me when I say I didn't plan it."

### Beat 5: Correct finale
*Narrator, after the accusation is validated:*
> "Ray Kowalski was taken into custody at twelve minutes past two in the morning. He didn't resist. The Bridgewater Inn went quiet — the kind of quiet that follows something that can't be unsaid. Gerald Fenn was not a good man, but he deserved a trial, not a verdict from a frightened one. Carol Wentworth left Millhaven three weeks later, on her own terms. Edna Marsh reopened the bar on Monday. Dennis Pruett was the first customer. Some things about a small town don't change. Some things do."

## 10. Period Constraints (for classifier and LLM)

Setting: October 1981, small-town New Hampshire.

**In-period and appropriate:**
- Reagan administration, recession anxiety, mill closures
- Rotary phones, payphones, no answering machines at the inn
- No personal computers, no internet, no mobile phones
- AM/FM radio, cassette tapes, 8-tracks still around
- TV in the bar (possibly a game on)
- Cars: American makes, maybe an older foreign car being unusual
- Drinks: domestic beer, well whiskey, gin and tonic
- Clothes: early 80s but not fashion-forward in a small town
- The Red Sox, the Patriots (bad years), local high school sports
- Vietnam is recent history; the war gets mentioned by Dennis's generation

**Out of period (classifier rejects):**
- Internet, email, websites, apps, smartphones
- Cable TV channels (HBO existed but was rare in small towns)
- Any post-1981 cultural reference
- Modern slang

## 11. Delivery Notes for TTS Production

- Ray's Tier 3 responses should be produced at a slower pace than his Tier 1 responses — the guard coming down costs him something.
- Edna always sounds like she's slightly more interested in what she's doing than in the conversation.
- Dennis trails off mid-sentence twice in every scene. This is a feature.
- Carol never uses contractions when she's being careful. She uses them when she's stopped being careful. That's the tell.
- The narrator pauses half a beat longer than feels natural before delivering bad news.

## 12. Paper Playtest — Fair-Play Verification

**Scenario: Player speaks to everyone, asks about Ray's movements.**
- Dennis confirms gap (Tier 2) ✓
- Edna confirms kitchen sighting (Tier 2) ✓
- Player connects back-stairs access to kitchen exit ✓
- Player asks Carol about Fenn knowing — gets motive (Tier 3) ✓
- Player confronts Ray — gets partial admission (Tier 3) ✓
- Accusation: Ray. Result: `ending-correct-full` ✓

**Scenario: Player fixates on Dwight.**
- Note found in Room 5 (Tier 1) ✓
- Edna confirms Dwight went upstairs (Tier 2) ✓
- Dennis confirms Dwight's presence (Tier 2) ✓
- But: Dwight's upstairs gap is only 5 minutes (Edna's testimony)
- Death window is 25–35 minutes
- 5-minute gap doesn't fit — player has to notice this contradiction
- If player doesn't notice: `ending-wrong-dwight` (fair — the red herring is designed to trap)
- If player notices: prompted to look for longer absences → Ray ✓

**Scenario: Player never speaks to Dennis.**
- Ray-was-away clue is unavailable from Dennis
- BUT: Edna's kitchen-sink clue alone is enough to raise the question
- Ray's back-stairs access creates suspicion
- Player can still reach `ending-correct-partial` with 4 clues
- `clue-ray-was-away` is not required — only 4 of 6 needed ✓

**Dead-end check:**
- Can a player be locked out of any required clue? No — all 6 clues are independently reachable through different conversation paths. ✓
- Can a player reach the minimum 4 without speaking to every character? Yes — Tier 1 clues + Edna + Ray is sufficient. ✓
