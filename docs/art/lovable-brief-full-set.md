## 3. The project, briefly

Radio Night is a voice-first interactive murder mystery played in a web browser. The first mystery, "The Sitting", is set on a rainy Saturday night in October 1981 at the Bridgewater Inn, a two-storey clapboard inn in Millhaven, a tired New Hampshire mill town. A portrait painter has been found dead in his room. The player questions the suspects by speaking to them, and the characters answer in voices.

Tone: warm, dim, quietly tense, like a late-night radio play. Family-friendly (PG-13): no gore, no violence shown, no visible injury. The pictures are a quiet companion to the sound.

## 4. The style (unchanged, locked)

A warm, grainy 1981 colour photograph. Colour negative film, warm tungsten light, visible grain, shallow depth of field. It must look photographed, not painted, drawn, rendered, 3D or "AI illustration".

**Style lock.** Begin every prompt with this exact sentence, unchanged:

> Photograph, 1981, colour negative film look, warm tungsten light, visible film grain, shallow depth of field, small-town New Hampshire.

End every prompt with: `No text, no logos, no watermark, nothing modern.`

Palette: deep charcoal browns and blacks, warm amber, aged cream, a little cool blue from a television or a rainy window. Nothing bright, neon or cold.

Era: strictly 1981. Wooden paneling, patterned carpet, old CRT television, rotary phone. No flat screens, LEDs, smartphones, modern clothes, modern cars or logos.

Never include text, letters, numbers, signs, logos or watermarks. Never include readable writing of any kind, even on bottle labels, doors or papers.

### How to use references

### Lessons from the pilot (please apply to everything)

- **Rooms are cropped.** The room banner is a wide strip: about 3:1 on a phone, up to about 5:1 on a tablet, and close to full 16:9 only on large screens. So keep everything important in a **horizontal band across the middle of the frame (about the middle 40 percent of the height)**, and keep the lowest quarter calm and dark. The room name will sit over it. In the Tap Room the television ended up above the band and was cropped out in the strip. Keep key objects lower than that.
- **No hidden clues in the rooms.** One object in this mystery is a heavy glass ashtray. No room picture may contain a glass ashtray, a loose drinking glass standing out on its own, or a loose sheet of paper, unless a prompt below says so. Ordinary bottles and shelf glasses behind a bar are fine. This keeps the pictures fair: a picture must never give away, or hide, a clue.
- **No people in room images.** They are empty.
- **No injury or distress** in any portrait.
